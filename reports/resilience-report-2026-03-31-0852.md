## Executive Summary

The dominant behavioral pattern on March 31 continues to reflect a population maintaining protective compliance and partial daily functioning under sustained multi-front missile threat, now entering its second month. Shelter-seeking behavior remains routine across the country, with field reports from northern border communities documenting granular differentiation: residents of Abdon and Netu'a remain in place near protected spaces, while younger cohorts have departed Goren and Shetula, leaving communities demographically thinned to older, less mobile residents who face the greatest shelter-access deficits. The most analytically significant structural split runs through functional continuity and wellbeing: national-level systems — compensation funds processing 21,552 claims, airport maintaining skeleton operations, Knesset approving retroactive unemployment frameworks — are functioning as designed, while at the community level in northern border settlements, shelter infrastructure is inadequate, welfare services cannot reach isolated elderly, volunteer corps are understaffed, and informal education has effectively ceased. This gap between national institutional response and local physical capacity is the defining structural condition of the current period.

## Components

**What each column means**

- **Assessment reliability** — How much we trust the **component score** (low / medium / high), based on both how strong the weighted evidence is and how broadly it appears across articles.
- **Evidence level** — A **0–100% certainty** score: how much *weighted* behavioral evidence we found for this component. **It is not “how good resilience is.”** High % means we had enough signal strength to be confident in the scoring step; the **Component score** (1–10) tells you favorable vs unfavorable.
- **Evidence base** — Number of behavioral signals tagged to this component.
- **Article coverage** — How many *distinct* articles in today’s sample contributed at least one signal, as a fraction of all articles analyzed.

**How Evidence level % is calculated (technical)** — Each signal adds `|mapping weight| × scope weight × reliability weight`. Those add up to *evidence mass*. Certainty = `1 − e^(−evidence_mass / 2)`, shown as %. It saturates toward 100% as mass grows. **Direction** (supporting vs opposing) is separate and shown under Evidence direction.

Functional continuity shows a pronounced split between national-level partial recovery and community-level structural breakdown. At the national level, indicators of partial normalization are present: Tel Aviv resuming parking enforcement, traffic volumes recovering, hotels filling for Passover, and essential services maintaining operations under adjusted conditions. Against this, the disruption is systemic and widening: 50,798 new job seekers registered since the operation began — disproportionately women forced home by school closures — manufacturing output is depressed across sectors, and Ben Gurion Airport operates at roughly 7% of normal flight volume. Field reports from northern border communities document a more severe and distinct failure mode: shelters were not designed for continuous occupancy and are now serving as de facto residential spaces, educational frameworks have ceased across multiple communities (Yaara, Karem Ben Zimra, Manot), agricultural activity is disrupted, and tourism is inactive. The healthcare system is under strain at both ends — hospitals at peak capacity in population centers, while Kiryat Shmona's emergency medical hotline was closed during the period of greatest need.

Two contradictory dynamics run alongside this solidarity: first, active social conflict and tension, including anti-war protest dispersals, shelter vandalism, and an armed incident at a checkpoint with subsequent threats against wounded soldiers; and second, a structural exclusion documented in both journalism and field reports — Arab Israeli communities in northern Israel face the same missile threat as Jewish communities but are nearly absent from media coverage and lack shelter infrastructure, while field reports from Horfish, Yaara, and Alkosh document elderly and special-needs residents experiencing effective social isolation as their frameworks have closed. Unmet wellbeing needs are documented across multiple population types and represent the most pervasive structural deficit in the current period. The elderly in northern border communities face a compound burden documented in field reports from at least twelve communities: physical inability to reach shelters within alert times, closure of social frameworks that previously provided contact and structure, and welfare services that cannot achieve systematic coverage — with Dafna lacking both an emergency team and a resilience team, and Psuta's volunteer clubs closed entirely. Former hostages represent a distinct high-acuity population: both Romi Gonen and Luis Har describe ongoing trauma activation triggered by sirens and sudden sounds, with no evidence of specialized structured response to this group's needs in current reporting.
