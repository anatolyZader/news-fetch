## Executive Summary

The dominant behavioral pattern on March 28 remains consistent with prior days: residents across multiple regions are actively entering protected spaces in response to sirens and following Home Front Command directives at high rates, with shelter use demonstrably preventing casualties — most visibly in the case of two children and babysitters who survived a direct cluster munition impact on a reinforced room in Petah Tikva. At the same time, a structurally significant gap in physical protection persists and is widening in salience: 55% of Israeli apartments lack a protected room (mamad), 50% of Qassam residents report the same, 41% of hospital operating rooms are unprotected, and entire neighborhoods in Samaria lack certified shelters — meaning the compliance behavior that is saving lives in protected spaces is unavailable to a substantial portion of the population. These two realities are not in tension; they coexist as a system in which behavioral compliance is high but the physical infrastructure that makes compliance effective is unevenly distributed.

Functional continuity shows a more complex picture than prior days: commercial and educational activity continues in parts of the country, with Dizengoff Center markets operating, academic frameworks adapted for reserve soldiers, and regulatory licenses extended by the Knesset — yet food assistance demand has risen 25% ahead of Passover, airport relaxations were cancelled, and mental health treatment continuity is disrupted for thousands. Community capital is under measurable pressure: volunteering and informal mutual aid are present but resource deficits — protective infrastructure, food access, logistical reach to the north — are documented at scale. Wellbeing signals, particularly for reserve soldiers, elderly hospital patients, adolescents, and residents without protected rooms, constitute the most concentrated area of unmet need across the day's reporting, continuing a declining trend visible since March 26.

## Components

**What each column means**

- **Assessment reliability** — How much we trust the **component score** (low / medium / high), based on both how strong the weighted evidence is and how broadly it appears across articles.
- **Evidence level** — A **0–100% certainty** score: how much *weighted* behavioral evidence we found for this component. **It is not “how good resilience is.”** High % means we had enough signal strength to be confident in the scoring step; the **Component score** (1–10) tells you favorable vs unfavorable.
- **Evidence base** — Number of behavioral signals tagged to this component.
- **Article coverage** — How many *distinct* articles in today’s sample contributed at least one signal, as a fraction of all articles analyzed.

**How Evidence level % is calculated (technical)** — Each signal adds `|mapping weight| × scope weight × reliability weight`. Those add up to *evidence mass*. Certainty = `1 − e^(−evidence_mass / 2)`, shown as %. It saturates toward 100% as mass grows. **Direction** (supporting vs opposing) is separate and shown under Evidence direction.
