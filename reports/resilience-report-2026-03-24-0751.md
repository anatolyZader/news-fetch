## Executive Summary

On March 24, the behavioral picture is defined by a functional split between protective compliance and systemic continuity. Shelter-seeking behavior remains prevalent — multiple reports confirm residents entering protected spaces during alerts, and in several documented cases their presence in shelters directly prevented casualties — yet a concurrent pattern of normalization-driven non-compliance is also present, with reports indicating that a substantial proportion of those injured in a missile event had not reached a protected space. Information dissemination from the Home Front Command is active and reaching mobile devices across relevant areas, and leadership communication scores notably higher than on prior days; however, these gains occur against a backdrop of deteriorating functional continuity, with educational activity prohibited nationwide following strikes on Dimona and Arad, airport capacity constrained, and food prices rising due to supply chain disruption. The trajectory from March 22–24 shows protective behavior holding relatively steady while functional continuity continues to decline, and wellbeing indicators — sleep disruption, anxiety medication demand, and shelter inadequacy — are trending negatively for the third consecutive day.

The most operationally significant split evident today is between populations who are physically protected and those whose daily-life infrastructure has fractured around them. Parents report that children lack routine while adult work schedules continue unchanged; shelter infrastructure in border communities remains physically inadequate in terms of capacity and basic services; and a hospital is fundraising for essential medical equipment for its underground facility — indicating that even acute healthcare continuity depends on ad hoc resource mobilization rather than standing provision. Community capital signals are mixed: individual actors such as a kindergarten owner mobilizing staff around protected-space guidelines demonstrate local adaptive capacity, but no evidence of coordinated multi-sector or authority-led community resource activation was present in today's sample. Belonging and solidarity produced no signals today, representing a sustained gap that may reflect reporting choices rather than an absence of mutual-aid behavior, but cannot be confirmed either way from available evidence.

## Components

**What each column means**

- **Assessment reliability** — How much we trust the **component score** (low / medium / high), based on both how strong the weighted evidence is and how broadly it appears across articles.
- **Evidence level** — A **0–100% certainty** score: how much *weighted* behavioral evidence we found for this component. **It is not “how good resilience is.”** High % means we had enough signal strength to be confident in the scoring step; the **Component score** (1–10) tells you favorable vs unfavorable.
- **Evidence base** — Number of behavioral signals tagged to this component.
- **Article coverage** — How many *distinct* articles in today’s sample contributed at least one signal, as a fraction of all articles analyzed.

**How Evidence level % is calculated (technical)** — Each signal adds `|mapping weight| × scope weight × reliability weight`. Those add up to *evidence mass*. Certainty = `1 − e^(−evidence_mass / 2)`, shown as %. It saturates toward 100% as mass grows. **Direction** (supporting vs opposing) is separate and shown under Evidence direction.
