## Executive Summary

The dominant behavioral pattern on April 1 represents a marked upward shift from the near-total functional collapse recorded on March 31, with narrative, information, lifesaving behavior, community capital, and leadership all scoring at or near 9/10. Field reports from trained resilience professionals visiting northern border communities — covering Baram, Hiram, Maaleh Yosef, Upper Galilee, and Mevuot Heron regions — document a population that has largely stabilized into an emergency routine: residents remain in or near shelters, informal activity frameworks are operating in the majority of visited settlements, and community coordinators and Home Front Command units are functioning in most locations. The Passover holiday context amplifies both directions simultaneously: on one hand, approximately 1,000 residents in northern communities registered for municipal Seder initiatives in shelters, Chabad organized 1,300 public Seders for 50,000 people, and consumer spending data showed a 5.5% increase on Passover eve relative to the prior year — behavioral indicators of a population sustaining cultural and social continuity under fire. On the other hand, structural conditions that were documented on March 31 remain largely unchanged: protection gaps for elderly and disabled residents are recorded across more than twenty visited communities, private daycare centers remain closed leaving thousands of parents without childcare, more than 50,000 new job seekers registered since the war began, and Q1 2026 GDP is projected at -9.5% annually.

The most analytically significant split visible across components is between populations who are well-served by current response structures and those who fall outside them. The Arab communities of Arabe al-Aramsha, Horfish, and Ghajar — documented by field professionals — show a pattern where 30–90% of homes lack individual safe rooms or functioning shelters, community frameworks for elderly and special-needs populations are largely absent, and children's activities remain closed. This contrasts with Jewish kibbutzim in the same geographic regions where HFC units are operational, informal education is running, and decompression activities are available. A parallel structural gap exists in the general population: workers in the private sector who returned to work during the prior Iran ceasefire are excluded from the current unemployment benefits framework, while more than 38,000 people have sought mental health services since the war began — a figure authorities describe as representing only a fraction of actual need. Functional continuity (2/10) and wellbeing at-risk (2/10) remain the two components with scores unchanged from the prior day, anchored by healthcare systems operating under sustained pressure, persistent shelter infrastructure deficits, and unmet needs among elderly, disabled, and economically vulnerable populations.

## Components

**What each column means**

- **Assessment reliability** — How much we trust the **component score** (low / medium / high), based on both how strong the weighted evidence is and how broadly it appears across articles.
- **Evidence level** — A **0–100% certainty** score: how much *weighted* behavioral evidence we found for this component. **It is not “how good resilience is.”** High % means we had enough signal strength to be confident in the scoring step; the **Component score** (1–10) tells you favorable vs unfavorable.
- **Evidence base** — Number of behavioral signals tagged to this component.
- **Article coverage** — How many *distinct* articles in today’s sample contributed at least one signal, as a fraction of all articles analyzed.

**How Evidence level % is calculated (technical)** — Each signal adds `|mapping weight| × scope weight × reliability weight`. Those add up to *evidence mass*. Certainty = `1 − e^(−evidence_mass / 2)`, shown as %. It saturates toward 100% as mass grows. **Direction** (supporting vs opposing) is separate and shown under Evidence direction.

Leadership functions at high capacity in most of the sampled contexts, with field reports documenting active local leadership in the majority of visited northern communities and national leadership demonstrating responsiveness — most concretely in the April 1 change to Iron Dome highway interception policy made directly in response to resident complaints, and the Prime Minister's emergency pre-Passover meeting on the employment framework.
