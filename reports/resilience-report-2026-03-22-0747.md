## Executive Summary

On March 22, the behavioral landscape is defined by two contradictory patterns operating simultaneously. Institutional systems — leadership communication, emergency guidance, and community resource mobilization — are functioning at a high level: the Home Front Command issued explicit, time-bounded restrictions across multiple southern regions, senior officials conducted visible on-site assessments, Soroka Hospital activated mass-casualty protocols, and the Arad municipality established a family registration and evacuation center within hours of the strikes. These responses represent continuity of the formal emergency management infrastructure documented in prior days. Against this, functional continuity and physical wellbeing have deteriorated materially: Iranian ballistic missiles struck Dimona and Arad directly, interceptors failed on at least two trajectories, 175 individuals were evacuated to Soroka Hospital including 18 children, all in-person education nationwide was suspended, and bus service continues at 60% capacity. The gap between institutional responsiveness and the scale of harm reaching residents — particularly children and vulnerable populations — defines the day's behavioral profile.
