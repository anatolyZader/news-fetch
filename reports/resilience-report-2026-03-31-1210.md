## Executive Summary

The dominant pattern emerging from field reports across more than twenty northern border communities is one of structural protection deficit compounded by fragmented coordination. Across settlements including Psuta, Horfish, Granot HaGalil, Kfar Giladi, and Ramot Naftali, between 30% and 55% of homes lack individual safe rooms, public shelters are in poor condition or inaccessible to elderly and mobility-limited residents, and Home Front Command cells are either unstaffed, missing a chair, or operating with a single trained volunteer. This is not a reporting artifact: the breadth and consistency of these findings across field visits constitutes a structural pattern. A secondary split runs through nearly every community between the socially integrated majority — who have some form of informal support, volunteer contact, or community activity — and isolated vulnerable populations (elderly living alone, special-needs individuals, women with partners in reserve service, bedridden patients) who are systematically not reached by welfare services, not mapped by HFC, and not covered by existing volunteer networks.

## Components

**What each column means**

- **Assessment reliability** — How much we trust the **component score** (low / medium / high), based on both how strong the weighted evidence is and how broadly it appears across articles.
- **Evidence level** — A **0–100% certainty** score: how much *weighted* behavioral evidence we found for this component. **It is not “how good resilience is.”** High % means we had enough signal strength to be confident in the scoring step; the **Component score** (1–10) tells you favorable vs unfavorable.
- **Evidence base** — Number of behavioral signals tagged to this component.
- **Article coverage** — How many *distinct* articles in today’s sample contributed at least one signal, as a fraction of all articles analyzed.

**How Evidence level % is calculated (technical)** — Each signal adds `|mapping weight| × scope weight × reliability weight`. Those add up to *evidence mass*. Certainty = `1 − e^(−evidence_mass / 2)`, shown as %. It saturates toward 100% as mass grows. **Direction** (supporting vs opposing) is separate and shown under Evidence direction.

Field report evidence from visited communities documents two contradictory behavioral patterns within the lifesaving component. Functional continuity is deeply compromised across the full range of visited northern border communities, with field reports from more than twenty settlements consistently documenting the same structural pattern: inadequate physical protection infrastructure, collapsed educational and social frameworks, and absent or understaffed HFC coordination. The split that best characterizes today's data is between communities where partial emergency routines have been established — such as Batzet, where some agricultural work and youth activities continue in cycles — and the majority of communities where no functioning routine has been achieved and daily life has effectively stopped for vulnerable populations. Education represents a particular failure mode: formal schooling is closed in multiple locations, Zoom substitutes are described as having limited success, and informal activities are either absent or operating under constraints that exclude populations without shelter access. Economic functioning is similarly disrupted, with small businesses at risk of collapse, tourism halted, and agriculture damaged — but no evidence of state guidance or support mechanisms reaching affected business owners was found in today's sample.

Community capital presents a pronounced split between a small number of communities with functioning volunteer networks and the majority where capital has been substantially depleted or was never adequately structured.
