## Executive Summary

On March 21, the dominant behavioral pattern across central Israel is one of compounding disruption following a cluster munition strike on a kindergarten in Rishon Lezion. Education Minister Kisch cancelled Passover holiday camps across 18 municipalities, and city managers in Rishon Lezion and elsewhere publicly declined to reopen schools until safety conditions are met — a leadership posture that is visible and consistent, though it deepens the functional disruption for families. Parents describe intrusive recollections of the strike, children exhibiting distress, and an inability to return to calm; a displaced population of more than 1,000 Beit Shemesh residents is attempting to reconstruct routine in hotel accommodation while reporting physical symptoms consistent with blast exposure. The day's signals show a meaningful divergence between components: local leadership is functioning and publicly communicative, while functional continuity, wellbeing response infrastructure, and the circulating community narrative have all deteriorated further from their already low baselines of March 19.
