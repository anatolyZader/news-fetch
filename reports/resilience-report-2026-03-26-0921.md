## Executive Summary

On March 26, the behavioral pattern established over prior days continues without meaningful change in direction: physical protective compliance remains high and well-documented, with shelter use directly linked to casualty prevention across multiple sites, while the structural systems on which daily life depends remain at reduced or disrupted capacity. The most consequential behavioral split of the day is between the protective behavior that is working — residents entering shelters, first responders reaching impact sites within minutes, emergency medical teams restoring cardiac function in children who collapsed during alerts — and the physical infrastructure that is failing: more than three million residents, approximately one-third of the population, lack standard shelter protection, and 41% of hospital operating rooms are unprotected. This structural deficit is not new; it is a persistent condition that the current tempo of missile fire is making actively consequential.

The wellbeing and functional continuity components remain at sustained low capacity for a third consecutive day, with no directional improvement visible. Food assistance demand is up 25%, prescription and over-the-counter anxiety and sleep medication consumption is measurably elevated, school systems remain closed, and the education closure alone is costing the economy an estimated 1.25 billion shekels per week. Against this, community-level solidarity behaviors are visible and increasing — volunteers packing food baskets under sirens, a community platform matching stranded travelers with Passover hosts, a newly established ultra-Orthodox emergency rescue unit — but these actions are absorbing need rather than resolving the structural gaps that generate it. Leadership outputs include a government aid approval of approximately 110 million shekels and acceleration of fortification work in border communities, but these measures are not yet visible in the daily experience of the population they address.

## Components

**What each column means**

- **Assessment reliability** — How much we trust the **component score** (low / medium / high), based on both how strong the weighted evidence is and how broadly it appears across articles.
- **Evidence level** — A **0–100% certainty** score: how much *weighted* behavioral evidence we found for this component. **It is not “how good resilience is.”** High % means we had enough signal strength to be confident in the scoring step; the **Component score** (1–10) tells you favorable vs unfavorable.
- **Evidence base** — Number of behavioral signals tagged to this component.
- **Article coverage** — How many *distinct* articles in today’s sample contributed at least one signal, as a fraction of all articles analyzed.

**How Evidence level % is calculated (technical)** — Each signal adds `|mapping weight| × scope weight × reliability weight`. Those add up to *evidence mass*. Certainty = `1 − e^(−evidence_mass / 2)`, shown as %. It saturates toward 100% as mass grows. **Direction** (supporting vs opposing) is separate and shown under Evidence direction.
