## Executive Summary

The dominant behavioral pattern on March 29 is a population that continues to comply with shelter directives at high rates and circulates a coherent coping narrative, while experiencing deepening functional collapse across daily life, economic continuity, and physical protection adequacy. Shelter compliance is documented across multiple communities and population types — from a high-tech worker maintaining employment from a shelter, to newborn parents rushing to a shelter minutes after a home delivery, to returned hostages at Kibbutz Gazit navigating repeated siren cycles — yet this compliance operates against a background of structural protection deficits that are both widening in geographic scope and becoming more granular in documentation. Expert field reports from northern border communities across Maaleh Yosef, Galil Elyon, Matte Asher, and Mevuot Heron regional councils provide primary-source evidence of populations largely absent from journalism: elderly residents unable to reach distant public shelters, Arab villages with inadequate shelter infrastructure, families with disabled members who cannot access organized protection, and Home Front Command volunteer arrays that are either understaffed or entirely absent in multiple communities. The gap between communities with strong local leadership and functioning volunteer systems and those without — documented explicitly in Batzet, Horfish, and Adamit versus Alkosh, Yaara, and Granot HaGalil — constitutes the most operationally significant split in today's data, as it determines whether vulnerable populations have any organized access to either physical protection or social support.

## Components

**What each column means**

- **Assessment reliability** — How much we trust the **component score** (low / medium / high), based on both how strong the weighted evidence is and how broadly it appears across articles.
- **Evidence level** — A **0–100% certainty** score: how much *weighted* behavioral evidence we found for this component. **It is not “how good resilience is.”** High % means we had enough signal strength to be confident in the scoring step; the **Component score** (1–10) tells you favorable vs unfavorable.
- **Evidence base** — Number of behavioral signals tagged to this component.
- **Article coverage** — How many *distinct* articles in today’s sample contributed at least one signal, as a fraction of all articles analyzed.

**How Evidence level % is calculated (technical)** — Each signal adds `|mapping weight| × scope weight × reliability weight`. Those add up to *evidence mass*. Certainty = `1 − e^(−evidence_mass / 2)`, shown as %. It saturates toward 100% as mass grows. **Direction** (supporting vs opposing) is separate and shown under Evidence direction.

Functional continuity has declined to its lowest recorded score, and the signal volume from both journalistic and field report sources — the largest of any component today — reflects widespread, multi-domain disruption rather than isolated incidents. Economic continuity is the most quantified failure: 50,798 new job seekers since late February, a 25% increase in registered unemployed in four days alone, with women disproportionately represented at 63.4% of new registrants due to school closure forcing them to remain at home with children. The community capital picture is structurally split between a national mobilization layer — compensation teams, Defense Ministry shelter programs, volunteer organizations, and cross-sector cultural initiatives — and a community-level implementation gap that is documented with specificity in field reports across more than fifteen northern border communities. At the national and regional level, resource mobilization is active: 74 compensation fund teams are deployed, 100 portable shelters are being distributed to Bedouin villages, and a Defense Ministry program covers 100 public shelters and approximately 3,000 private safe rooms. At the community level, field reports identify that Home Front Command volunteer systems are either absent, incomplete, or so thinly staffed as to be operationally ineffective: in Yaara, 5 of over 250 families have been reached by volunteers; in Alkosh, only a single coordinator exists with no additional functionaries; in Granot HaGalil, no body has conducted a systematic mapping of elderly and special-needs residents. The communities where community capital is functioning — Batzet, Psuta, Adamit, Manot — share the presence of an active volunteer array and, in several cases, a functioning community coordinator role. Those where it is failing share the common characteristic of role absence at the community level, indicating that the national mobilization layer does not automatically translate into local protection.
