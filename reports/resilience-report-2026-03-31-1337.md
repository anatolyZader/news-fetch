## Executive Summary

The dominant behavioral pattern on March 31 is one of near-total functional collapse in daily life across northern border communities, documented through field reports covering populations — elderly residents, Arab villages, small kibbutzim, and special-needs individuals — who are largely absent from news coverage. Across more than twenty visited communities in the Baram, Hiram, and Maaleh Yosef regions, field professionals consistently recorded the same structural conditions: the majority of residents remain confined to their homes with minimal street movement, formal educational and social frameworks are closed or operating only minimally via Zoom, agriculture is severely constrained by inadequate field protection, and elderly and disabled residents face a compound exposure — they lack individual safe rooms, public shelters are distant or in poor condition, and alert times are too short to reach protection. The split between communities with functional volunteer corps and those without is analytically significant: where HFC volunteer units and strong local leadership are active (Batzet, Shtula, Egger, Adamit), informal children's activities and outreach to vulnerable populations continue at a reduced level; where volunteer infrastructure is absent or understrength (Granot HaGalil, Alkosh, Dafna, Aven Menachem), no structured need-mapping exists and welfare services have lost contact with the populations they serve.

## Components

**What each column means**

- **Assessment reliability** — How much we trust the **component score** (low / medium / high), based on both how strong the weighted evidence is and how broadly it appears across articles.
- **Evidence level** — A **0–100% certainty** score: how much *weighted* behavioral evidence we found for this component. **It is not “how good resilience is.”** High % means we had enough signal strength to be confident in the scoring step; the **Component score** (1–10) tells you favorable vs unfavorable.
- **Evidence base** — Number of behavioral signals tagged to this component.
- **Article coverage** — How many *distinct* articles in today’s sample contributed at least one signal, as a fraction of all articles analyzed.

**How Evidence level % is calculated (technical)** — Each signal adds `|mapping weight| × scope weight × reliability weight`. Those add up to *evidence mass*. Certainty = `1 − e^(−evidence_mass / 2)`, shown as %. It saturates toward 100% as mass grows. **Direction** (supporting vs opposing) is separate and shown under Evidence direction.

A consistent pattern of information deprivation is documented across both resident quotations and field professional observations. Residents describe a subjective condition of exclusion from decision-relevant knowledge, using the framing of being 'extras' without knowledge of 'the film' — indicating not confusion about specific instructions but a more fundamental absence of situational framing from authorities. The split between information presence and information effectiveness is pronounced: media content is available and consumed in volume, yet residents report that increased consumption produces less understanding rather than more, suggesting that broadcast information is not translated into locally actionable guidance. No evidence was found of communication mechanisms adapted to specific population groups such as elderly residents, Arab villages during Ramadan, or families with young children — an informative absence given the documented diversity of populations in the field reports.

### 🛡️ Effective Life-Saving Behavior
*התנהגות אפקטיבית להצלת חיים*

**Assessment reliability:** medium *(based on how much evidence was found and how broadly it appears across the sample)* | **Evidence level:** 53% *(certainty % from weighted evidence mass—not the same as “good” or “bad” resilience; use Component score for direction)*
**Evidence base:** 7 behavioral signals found in 5 of 67 articles *(7.5% of today's sample, very low spread across sources)* | **Evidence direction:** more supporting than opposing *(supporting: 0.8, opposing: 0.7)*

Shelter-in-place behavior is consistently observed across visited communities, with residents remaining in or near protected spaces and minimizing outdoor movement — behavioral evidence of compliance with Home Front Command protective guidance. Field reports from Abdon and Netu'a document this pattern as a community-wide norm rather than isolated individual behavior. A compliance split is present within the Netu'a data: established adult residents are choosing to remain, while parents with children face an internal household disagreement, with parents seeking evacuation and children refusing — indicating that the compliance decision is not uniform even within families. A risk-exposure behavior was observed in Arrabe al-Aramsha, where residents were filmed recording alerts rather than sheltering, representing documented non-compliance. The short alert times reported at multiple communities (Netu'a, Kibbutz Snir) constitute a structural constraint on compliance: even residents who intend to shelter may be physically unable to reach protected spaces in the available time, particularly elderly individuals.

Functional continuity across the visited northern communities is assessed as having collapsed across most dimensions of daily life, consistent with the score of 1/10 and the sustained declining trend from prior days. Formal educational frameworks are closed or suspended across the large majority of communities; where remote learning via Zoom has been attempted (Batzet), field professionals assess its success as limited. Agriculture continues in some locations but is constrained by inadequate field protection infrastructure — a structural gap affecting economic continuity in communities where farming constitutes primary livelihood. A differential is visible between communities with active HFC volunteer corps and strong local leadership (Batzet, Abdon, Egger), where informal children's activities and some degree of work continuity are maintained, and communities with under-resourced or absent volunteer infrastructure (Granot HaGalil, Aven Menachem, Manot), where no structured activity of any kind is reported. Healthcare continuity represents an additional gap: Kibbutz Ayalon's local clinic has no protection and medical response has not been restored, and elderly bedridden patients across multiple communities are described as unable to access any protective or medical support.

Community capital presents a pronounced split between communities where volunteer infrastructure is active and those where it has either not been established or has degraded below functional threshold. Field reports from Shtula, Egger, Adamit, and Liman document functioning volunteer corps conducting outreach to elderly and vulnerable residents, coordinating medicine and food delivery, and operating trained emergency units. External volunteers are supplementing internal capacity at Shtula, indicating that the gap is partially recognized at a regional level, but the scale of unmet need documented across communities substantially exceeds the documented volunteer capacity.

Leadership functioning presents two contradictory patterns across visited communities. In Batzet and Horfish, field professionals observe active, competent local leadership: Horfish's regional authority is described as broadcasting resilience and maintaining policy discipline, while Batzet's settlement is assessed as coping through cooperation among role holders. Against this, Aven Menachem's residents express deep distrust in the council, and resident quotations captured in the data describe a subjective experience of being excluded from authority communication — a behavioral manifestation of perceived leadership absence rather than presence. A structural leadership problem is documented at Kibbutz Snir, where shared military and civilian command has not been separated, creating a coordination failure that field professionals assess as requiring resolution. No evidence was found of leadership actively promoting HFC compliance messaging in public-facing form — a likely reporting gap given the field-report methodology, but an absence worth noting given the documented information gaps residents report.

### ❤️ Physical and Mental Wellbeing (At-Risk Populations)

Parents with small children represent a third population category: documented at Adamit as exhausted but not using available resilience services, suggesting that service awareness or access barriers exist even where services nominally exist.
