## Executive Summary

The dominant behavioral pattern on March 29 is one of sustained protective compliance alongside deepening structural strain. Residents across multiple regions continue to enter shelters on alert, follow Home Front Command directives, and maintain partial daily functioning — with individual cases such as a newborn rushed to shelter minutes after delivery and two children surviving a direct cluster munition impact on a reinforced room illustrating how compliance is actively preventing casualties. A pronounced positive narrative is circulating, with public figures, soldiers, returned hostages, and content creators all articulating frameworks of purpose, coping, and continuity. However, this surface-level functional coherence obscures three structural fissures visible in the data: first, 50,798 residents have registered as job seekers since Operation Lion's Roar began — 63.4% of them women, many displaced from employment by school closures — pointing to an economic disruption that official guidance has not addressed; second, Arab communities in northern Israel and unrecognized Bedouin villages in the Negev are documented as operating without protected rooms, public shelters, or organized evacuation routes, placing them outside the protection framework that the dominant narrative assumes; and third, the Yemeni missile threat introduces a reduced warning window that existing shelter-entry behavior was not calibrated for, creating a functional gap in an otherwise well-rehearsed response system.

Community capital presents a structural split: state mobilization (renovation of over 100 northern shelters, 3,000 new safe rooms planned, 74 compensation teams deployed, 100 portable shelters for Bedouin villages) is proceeding at documented scale, while grassroots voluntarism — shelter performances, tiramisu delivery by bicycle, LGBTQ+ youth volunteering in hospital basements, mixed Jewish-Arab emergency response teams — continues to function as a secondary support layer. The wellbeing picture is the most concerning component: 5,768 hospitalizations since the operation began, 24 anxiety casualties in a single 24-hour period, former hostages describing uncontrollable fear responses triggered by sirens, children defined as trauma casualties after shrapnel incidents, families self-isolating for over four weeks, and at-risk youth frameworks reporting staff shortages driven by low wages and emotional exhaustion. The belonging and solidarity component has declined directionally: protest dispersals with tear gas and arrests in Tel Aviv and Haifa, shelter vandalism in Tiberias, and documented social isolation among Kiryat Shmona residents all indicate fractures beneath the cohesion narrative. Leadership receives mixed signals — Home Front Command is operationally visible and credible, but coordination failures in military rehabilitation, residents rejecting Health Ministry declarations in Kiryat Shmona, and a blocked Palm Sunday ceremony that had been pre-coordinated point to gaps in institutional responsiveness.

## Components

**What each column means**

- **Assessment reliability** — How much we trust the **component score** (low / medium / high), based on both how strong the weighted evidence is and how broadly it appears across articles.
- **Evidence level** — A **0–100% certainty** score: how much *weighted* behavioral evidence we found for this component. **It is not “how good resilience is.”** High % means we had enough signal strength to be confident in the scoring step; the **Component score** (1–10) tells you favorable vs unfavorable.
- **Evidence base** — Number of behavioral signals tagged to this component.
- **Article coverage** — How many *distinct* articles in today’s sample contributed at least one signal, as a fraction of all articles analyzed.

**How Evidence level % is calculated (technical)** — Each signal adds `|mapping weight| × scope weight × reliability weight`. Those add up to *evidence mass*. Certainty = `1 − e^(−evidence_mass / 2)`, shown as %. It saturates toward 100% as mass grows. **Direction** (supporting vs opposing) is separate and shown under Evidence direction.
