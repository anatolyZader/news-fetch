## Executive Summary

Field reports from trained resilience professionals visiting northern border communities document a population that is largely remaining in place and maintaining an emergency routine, but doing so under conditions of significant physical vulnerability and psychological erosion. Across settlements in Ma'ale Yosef, Mateh Asher, and Galil Elyon sub-districts, a consistent structural pattern emerges: communities with strong informal leadership and functioning Home Front Command (HFC) volunteer corps are holding, while communities lacking both — such as Alkosh, Karem Ben Zimra, and Even Menahem — show apathy, social isolation, and deepening erosion. The split between the shelter-compliant majority in central Israel and the physically unprotected elderly in northern border villages is the most operationally consequential differential observed across components. Shelter compliance among the general population reached millions during Iranian barrages, but elderly residents in settlements such as Abdon, Goren, and Ramot Naftali cannot reach shelters within available warning times — a failure mode that affects a discrete and identifiable population not captured by aggregate compliance figures.

## Components

**What each column means**

- **Assessment reliability** — How much we trust the **component score** (low / medium / high), based on both how strong the weighted evidence is and how broadly it appears across articles.
- **Evidence level** — A **0–100% certainty** score: how much *weighted* behavioral evidence we found for this component. **It is not “how good resilience is.”** High % means we had enough signal strength to be confident in the scoring step; the **Component score** (1–10) tells you favorable vs unfavorable.
- **Evidence base** — Number of behavioral signals tagged to this component.
- **Article coverage** — How many *distinct* articles in today’s sample contributed at least one signal, as a fraction of all articles analyzed.

**How Evidence level % is calculated (technical)** — Each signal adds `|mapping weight| × scope weight × reliability weight`. Those add up to *evidence mass*. Certainty = `1 − e^(−evidence_mass / 2)`, shown as %. It saturates toward 100% as mass grows. **Direction** (supporting vs opposing) is separate and shown under Evidence direction.

### 📖 Narrative
*נרטיב*

Two contradictory narratives operate simultaneously across the populations observed. Field reports from northern border communities document a coping discourse — expressed through functioning HFC volunteer corps, returned populations, and community communication described as free of toxic discourse — existing alongside a narrative of abandonment and erosion in communities where physical infrastructure, leadership, and cohesion are absent. The authority narrative has sustained a measurable rupture following the IDF briefing that Hezbollah disarmament is not a current war goal: regional council heads describe this as a 'resounding slap in the face,' and residents report that promises of a quiet, secure border were 'unrealistic' from the start. This trust breakdown is not uniform — communities with strong internal leadership such as Mattat and Kibbutz Dan maintain a functioning coping narrative — but in communities such as Even Menahem and Karem Ben Zimra, residents describe themselves as abandoned and 'the periphery of the periphery.' No evidence was found of a coping narrative that reflects the full range of affected populations, including the elderly, Arab villages, and special-needs communities.

Large-scale shelter compliance was observed across central Israel and the north during multiple barrages, with millions entering protected spaces in response to alerts and documented cases of families reaching shelters seconds before missile impacts. Field reports from northern border communities confirm a complementary pattern: in settlements with sufficient warning time, residents remain near protected spaces throughout the day, effectively self-restricting movement as a continuous protective behavior. Non-compliance is documented in two structurally distinct modes: institutional non-compliance, typified by the Rashbi site operating without HFC authorization, and economic-driven non-compliance, where business operators describe shelter closure as financially unsustainable and resume operations with customers present in unprotected venues. A third mode — risk exposure due to guideline-reality mismatch — is captured by the sports team travel case and by residents in Arab villages who film during alerts, suggesting that for some sub-populations, guidelines are perceived as either impractical or not personally applicable.

Functional continuity shows a pronounced split between national infrastructure — which is partially resuming (cinema chains reopening, Ben Gurion Airport passenger quotas increasing, light rail preparing to resume) — and community-level daily life in northern border settlements, where the picture is one of widespread disruption. Field reports document that educational frameworks are closed or reduced to remote operation across most surveyed communities, with children and youth at home and informal education available only in fragments; the elderly and special-needs populations face closed clubs and social isolation. At the national level, missile strikes on Rosh Ha'ayin caused power outages, a building collapsed in Ramat Gan, and an industrial facility in Neot Hovav was set on fire — each representing acute but localized service disruption that emergency services addressed. The extension of reserve duty from six to nine weeks adds an additional strain on household and business functioning, particularly for essential workers whose childcare and household continuity are already disrupted. No evidence was found of a systematic response to the economic continuity needs of small business owners or to essential workers lacking childcare solutions.

Community capital operates across a wide range, from well-organized mutual aid — exemplified by Gaza envelope kibbutzim hosting 35 northern residents for Passover at community expense — to communities where the HFC volunteer corps consists of a single coordinator with no additional functionaries. ### 🔗 Belonging and Solidarity
*שייכות וסולידריות*

**Assessment reliability:** high *(based on how much evidence was found and how broadly it appears across the sample)* | **Evidence level:** 98% *(certainty % from weighted evidence mass—not the same as “good” or “bad” resilience; use Component score for direction)*
**Evidence base:** 29 behavioral signals found in 21 of 440 articles *(4.8% of today's sample, very low spread across sources)* | **Evidence direction:** evenly split *(supporting: 3.8, opposing: 3.8)*

Concrete mutual aid is present and active: cross-community hosting for Passover, coordinated food and medicine deliveries to elderly residents, community fundraising for damaged businesses, and a mayor organizing a community Seder for hard-hit neighboring settlements all constitute evidenced solidarity behavior. Simultaneously, several signals document fractures in belonging: the demonstration outside an Arab-Israeli journalist's home, reports of looting of missile-damaged properties (220 police cases opened), and a hate-vandalism incident on a community shelter indicate antisocial behaviors that exploit emergency conditions.
