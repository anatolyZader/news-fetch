## Executive Summary

On March 23, the dominant behavioral pattern is a widening gap between physical protective compliance — which remains relatively high — and the functional systems that sustain daily life, which are under pronounced strain. Residents across the country are entering shelters, following Home Front Command instructions, and in many cases actively improvising protective arrangements; however, the infrastructure supporting normal life has fractured across multiple domains simultaneously. Schools remain closed or open only partially and inconsistently, with approximately 170,000 workers absent from employment for economic reasons in February alone and 31,122 new job seekers registered since the start of Operation Lion's Roar. Aviation has effectively contracted to a fraction of normal capacity, with departing passengers from Ben Gurion down 85% since March 5, disrupting Passover travel for tens of thousands. The cumulative weight of these disruptions is registering in mental health indicators: crisis line calls to Natal increased 620% within 48 hours of the operation's start, and individual testimony describes sustained psychological deterioration over months rather than a single acute episode.

## Components

**What each column means**

- **Assessment reliability** — How much we trust the **component score** (low / medium / high), based on both how strong the weighted evidence is and how broadly it appears across articles.
- **Evidence level** — A **0–100% certainty** score: how much *weighted* behavioral evidence we found for this component. **It is not “how good resilience is.”** High % means we had enough signal strength to be confident in the scoring step; the **Component score** (1–10) tells you favorable vs unfavorable.
- **Evidence base** — Number of behavioral signals tagged to this component.
- **Article coverage** — How many *distinct* articles in today’s sample contributed at least one signal, as a fraction of all articles analyzed.

**How Evidence level % is calculated (technical)** — Each signal adds `|mapping weight| × scope weight × reliability weight`. Those add up to *evidence mass*. Certainty = `1 − e^(−evidence_mass / 2)`, shown as %. It saturates toward 100% as mass grows. **Direction** (supporting vs opposing) is separate and shown under Evidence direction.

Two contradictory behavioral narratives are simultaneously active in the public sphere. A resilience-oriented narrative — voiced by public figures such as Miriam Peretz, cited researchers, and institutional representatives — frames the current period as consistent with historical Jewish endurance and emphasizes social cohesion, shared meaning, and continued functioning. This narrative is supported by the observed temporary decline in suspected suicide cases during wartime, a pattern attributed to collective mobilization. However, a competing narrative of exhaustion and abandonment is also articulated, most directly by northern business owners and long-displaced residents who describe the cumulative effect of repeated rounds of conflict as incompatible with sustainable life: 'Can't live like this, can't plan anything.' ([source](https://www.maariv.co.il/lifestyle/travel/article-1299841)) The resilience narrative appears to be concentrated among nationally prominent voices and certain community institutions, while the counter-narrative is most pronounced among economically affected and geographically peripheral populations. No evidence was found of residents explicitly evaluating the credibility of official authority narratives against their own lived experience, which likely reflects a reporting gap rather than the absence of such assessments.
