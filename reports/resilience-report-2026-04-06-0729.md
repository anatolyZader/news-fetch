## Executive Summary

Across the eight components on April 6, the dominant behavioral pattern is a population under sustained and direct physical threat managing an emergency routine that is structurally incomplete in multiple dimensions. Nationally, the day was marked by a ballistic missile strike on a residential building in Haifa — producing two fatalities, two missing individuals, and a multi-hour rescue operation — alongside cluster munition impacts across at least fifteen sites in central Israel. Field reports from trained resilience professionals visiting northern border communities (Braeam, Maaleh Yosef, Hiram, Mevuot Hermon districts) document a parallel and more chronic condition: residents in the majority of visited settlements are remaining in place and following shelter directives when sirens sound, but are doing so in structurally deficient conditions — without individual safe rooms, with public shelters in disrepair, and with elderly and special-needs populations systematically outside the reach of welfare and HFC mapping systems. The narrative and leadership components remain relatively functional, while functional continuity and wellbeing scores remain at or near floor levels, consistent with the prior reporting period.

Two structural splits recur across multiple components. First, protection infrastructure is present and adequate for a subset of residents — typically those who can afford individual safe rooms or live in newer structures — while a separate population, concentrated among the elderly, low-income households, Arab villages (notably Ej'jar at 90% without safe rooms or shelter), and expansion neighborhoods of kibbutzim, faces near-total physical exposure with no viable alternative. Second, informal and volunteer-based community systems are functioning in many settlements — WhatsApp networks, HFC volunteer corps, decompression activities — but formal welfare systems are systematically disconnected from the populations most in need: Granot HaGalil's welfare services are reported as not connected to elderly or disabled residents; Manara's welfare department is unstaffed; Margelit's elderly residents express feeling like targets with no pathway out of exposure. The information and leadership components show continuity of official guidance delivery, but multiple communities explicitly requested basic clarification on what is and is not permitted — indicating that guidance presence and guidance sufficiency remain separate conditions.

**What each column means**

- **Assessment reliability** — How much we trust the **component score** (low / medium / high), based on both how strong the weighted evidence is and how broadly it appears across articles.
- **Evidence level** — A **0–100% certainty** score: how much *weighted* behavioral evidence we found for this component. **It is not “how good resilience is.”** High % means we had enough signal strength to be confident in the scoring step; the **Component score** (1–10) tells you favorable vs unfavorable.
- **Evidence base** — Number of behavioral signals tagged to this component.
- **Article coverage** — How many *distinct* articles in today’s sample contributed at least one signal, as a fraction of all articles analyzed.

**How Evidence level % is calculated (technical)** — Each signal adds `|mapping weight| × scope weight × reliability weight`. Those add up to *evidence mass*. Certainty = `1 − e^(−evidence_mass / 2)`, shown as %. It saturates toward 100% as mass grows. **Direction** (supporting vs opposing) is separate and shown under Evidence direction.

## Detailed Analysis

### 📖 Narrative
*נרטיב*

**Assessment reliability:** high *(based on how much evidence was found and how broadly it appears across the sample)* | **Evidence level:** 98% *(certainty % from weighted evidence mass—not the same as “good” or “bad” resilience; use Component score for direction)*
**Evidence base:** 21 behavioral signals found in 21 of 263 articles *(8.0% of today's sample, very low spread across sources)* | **Evidence direction:** more supporting than opposing *(supporting: 5.1, opposing: 2.5)*

Two contradictory behavioral narratives coexist across northern border communities on this date, with no convergence between them. Field reports from trained resilience professionals document a majority of visited settlements where residents describe themselves as coping — maintaining informal routines, staying in place, and drawing on community structures — with Kibbutz Dan, Manara, Kibbutz Snir, and Kfar Giladi all showing active coping discourse and mutual aid networks. Against this, a distinct cluster of communities — Shetula, Aven Menachem, Margelit, and Moshav Avivim — surfaces narratives of abandonment, burnout among role-holders, and declining community cohesion, with residents explicitly questioning whether the state's assurances reflect their lived conditions. The coping narrative is not population-wide: field reports consistently show that communities where the religious or professional core has departed (Shetula, Alkosh) report weaker collective narratives, while those retaining a high proportion of permanent residents show stronger ones. No evidence was found of residents expressing that the official authority narrative is credible and reflects their reality — a manifestation expected under current conditions but absent from today's signals.

Information and communication shows a split between channel availability and channel sufficiency. Compliance with protective directives is present across multiple documented incidents but is neither uniform nor structurally supported for all populations. Residents in adequately protected buildings followed directives and survived the Haifa strike; individual residents complied on roadsides and in vehicles. Functional continuity remains at a low level, with disruptions documented across education, employment, social services, and infrastructure. The education system is formally closed through at least April 12, with a partial framework under development for protected-space learning that the Ministry of Education and Home Front Command acknowledge will not cover the full student population. Employment disruption is extensive: 142,209 new job seekers since the operation began, with mothers of young children disproportionately represented — their share rising from 29% of new registrants in week one to 43% by week three. The critical split in this component is between communities with adequate shelter infrastructure, where modified routines are possible, and those — including the majority of Arab villages in the sample — where structural protection deficits make any outdoor or communal activity functionally unavailable.
