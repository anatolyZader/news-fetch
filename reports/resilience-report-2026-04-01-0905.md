## Executive Summary

Field reports from trained resilience professionals visiting northern border communities across the Baram, Hiram, and Maaleh Yosef regions document a population that has largely withdrawn from public life: streets are empty, businesses are closed, and residents remain confined to their homes across more than twenty visited communities. The dominant structural condition is a protection gap affecting a large proportion of households — in Psuta, 55% of homes lack protection; in Ghajar, 90% of residents have neither a safe room nor access to a shelter; in Yaara, the Home Front Command has reached only 5 of more than 250 families in its population survey. Functional continuity and wellbeing for at-risk populations score at the floor, driven by the convergence of absent or degraded physical protection, closed educational and social frameworks, and a volunteer infrastructure that is thin, undertrained, or entirely absent in several communities. Elderly residents, people with disabilities, and residents of Arab villages represent the most systematically underserved sub-populations: welfare services are not consistently connected to these groups, designated responsible persons for their care are absent in multiple communities, and physical shelter access is frequently unreachable within alert timeframes.
