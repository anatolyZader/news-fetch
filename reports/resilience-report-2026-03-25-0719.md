## Executive Summary

On March 25, the behavioral picture is defined by two contradictory patterns operating simultaneously and at scale. Physical protective compliance remains high and demonstrably effective — residents entered shelters, casualties were prevented in multiple incidents, and IDF confirmed that those who followed instructions survived strikes in Tel Aviv, Nesher, and Dimona — yet the population doing so is experiencing progressive deterioration across nearly every domain supporting daily life. Educational frameworks remain closed or operating only in limited, inconsistent form; aviation has contracted to a degree that airlines describe as effective closure of Israeli airspace; 31,122 new job seekers have registered since the operation began; and crisis line calls surged 620% within 48 hours of Operation Mighty Lion's start. The gap between physical protective behavior and functional daily life, present since March 23, has not narrowed — it has deepened.

A second pattern visible across March 25 is the substitution of state-level functional capacity by local, informal, and civil-society actors. Municipal leaders describe entering 'under the radar' to fill state gaps; 7,000 volunteers assisted in 3,500 damaged homes; the Golan Regional Council mobilized 170 resident volunteers including 80% of its kindergarten teachers to run an emergency education system; and individual community members improvised responses — from kindergarten managers delivering food to children's homes to municipalities distributing signs inviting neighbors to share their shelter space. This substitution pattern is broad and active, but it is managing a widening deficit rather than closing it: more than 3 million residents lack adequate shelter protection, 714,000 cannot reach a protected space at all, and unmet mental health and wellbeing needs are accumulating without a system-level response.
