# HFC field reporting guidelines (controlled corpus)

Population Behavior Officer (PBO) reports feed Pikud Ha-Home-Front **8-component** community resilience assessment. This document is the reference for separating **observation** from **interpretation**.

## Observation vs interpretation

- **Observation:** What was seen or heard — behavior, place, affected population, scale, channel, time window. Use concrete verbs and counts when available.
- **Interpretation:** Possible drivers or meaning — must be labeled as hypothesis, with alternatives left open. Never state interpretation as fact.

Reject reports that only score resilience, only quote officials without civilian behavior, or invent localities not mentioned in dialogue.

## Eight components (brief)

| Component | Field officers document |
|-----------|-------------------------|
| narrative | Dominant stories residents tell; competing narratives; trust in official messaging |
| information_communication | Channels (Pikud, municipality, media, social); clarity; rumors |
| lifesaving_behavior | Shelter entry, evacuation compliance, threat perception, access barriers |
| functional_continuity | Disruption to work, school, commerce, essential services |
| community_capital | Mutual aid, volunteers, coordination between actors |
| leadership | Trust or criticism toward formal/informal leaders; visible presence |
| belonging_solidarity | Solidarity vs exclusion; mutual aid examples |
| wellbeing_at_risk | Vulnerable groups; type of distress; whether needs are met |

## Spread and source basis (closed vocabulary)

- **Spread:** isolated | noticeable | widespread — must be supported by evidence in text.
- **Source basis:** direct (officer saw) | staff | residents | mixed — do not infer direct observation from hearsay.

## Pikud / HFC context

Reports may reference Home Front Command alerts, shelter instructions, and municipal welfare. Use these only when the officer or residents mentioned them; do not add alert types or instructions not in the source dialogue.

## Quality bar for approved drafts

Approved Hebrew field prose should: (1) lead with observation, (2) state spread and source basis, (3) end with a cautious interpretation cue, (4) stay within 2–4 sentences unless the dialogue requires more detail.
