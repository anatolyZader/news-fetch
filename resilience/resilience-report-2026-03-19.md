## Executive Summary

On March 19, the dominant behavioral reality remains one of acute and sustained distress, with residents across Israel experiencing sleep deprivation, panic responses, and the physical and psychological exhaustion of nearly three weeks under missile fire. The community health network 'Like Me' reports residents asking 'How do you find the strength to get out of bed?' and 'What do you do with the fact that my symptoms got so much worse?', while Kiryat Shmona residents describe 'fear, exhaustion and despair' and a sense that 'something can and should change, but in practice it does not happen.' Functional life has been deeply disrupted — airlines have cancelled flights through Passover, courts are in emergency mode, gas extraction has been halted for over two weeks at a cost of roughly 600 million shekels, and 40% of small businesses in at least one northern city have shut down permanently. Against this, pockets of genuine resilience are visible: sheltering compliance appears high and has likely saved lives, community capital is extraordinarily active with pharmacists delivering medications door-to-door, hundreds of thousands of reservists mobilizing, and KKL-JNF approving emergency funding for border communities.

### 🛡️ Effective Life-Saving Behavior

**Score:** 8/10 — 🟢 Good &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Confidence:** high &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Signals:** 18

Sheltering compliance is broadly evident and demonstrably saving lives — train station staff evacuated dozens of passengers seconds before a missile struck a platform ([source](https://www.ynet.co.il/wheels/article/sychfy00c11l)), and multiple families describe entering safe rooms within moments of alerts, sustaining property damage but no casualties ([source](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/hj4sydk5bg)). However, the system is showing stress fractures: a significant decline in nighttime shelter use in underground light rail stations has been documented ([source](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/sks0j8y9bx)), and at least one worker was killed specifically because he was not in a protected space when a missile struck ([source](https://www.haaretz.co.il/news/politics/war-2023/2026-03-19/ty-article-live/0000019d-0314-d46f-affd-939ee15f0000?liveBlogItemId=363611692)). The cumulative toll of compliance-related injury is significant: 718 people have been hurt traveling to shelters since the operation began, suggesting that the physical act of seeking protection carries its own risk ([source](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001537894)). Compliance fatigue — particularly overnight — is emerging as a growing concern consistent with the pattern of sustained alerts over nearly three weeks.
