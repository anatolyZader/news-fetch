## Executive Summary

Across all eight components, the dominant behavioral reality is one of acute distress, disruption, and fragmented coping under sustained missile fire. Residents are screaming in terror at impact sounds, losing consciousness while rushing to shelters, causing and being injured in panic-driven road accidents, and watching their children regress into bedwetting and nightly panic. Three million children have been confined to homes for over two weeks, schools are suspended or operating over Zoom for those with devices, 790 people were injured reaching shelters in a single 24-hour period, and at least 63 children have been unintentionally injured in accidents since the campaign began — four fatally. Structural failures compound individual distress: 35–40% of people with disabilities cannot access shelters, Holocaust survivors are giving up on healthcare access, and crane operators remain exposed at height with no protected space and no state response. The coping narrative is not circulating as effective — parents are explicitly rejecting resilience framing, asking 'will we wake up in panic again tonight,' and a regional council head is publicly stating that trauma 'will emerge after this war in a very significant way.'

## Detailed Analysis

### 📖 Narrative
*נרטיב*

**Score:** 1/10 — 🔴 Critical &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Confidence:** high &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Signals:** 24

The dominant narrative circulating among residents is not one of effective coping but of sustained, unresolved trauma. Children are asking parents 'will we wake up in panic again tonight,' and parents report being too exhausted to provide emotional responses, actively contradicting any official framing of resilience. A regional council head publicly stated that psychological damage 'will emerge after this war in a very significant way,' signaling that even leadership voices are not projecting a successful coping story. Isolated counter-signals exist — a bat mitzvah held after a family's home was destroyed, and a survey showing many Israelis feel safer at home than abroad — but these represent pockets of meaning-making rather than a population-wide narrative of efficacy. The coping story, where it exists, reflects only a subset of the population and is explicitly challenged by the lived experience voiced by residents and families.

**Positive behavioral signals:**
- Parents report children asking 'will we wake up in panic again tonight,' indicating rejection of resilience framing and ongoing trauma
- Regional council head: 'The psychological state of residents on the confrontation line. The trauma of residents here will emerge after this war in a very significant way'
- Residents describe 'endless runs to shelters' and fear that further evacuation will 'bury the city forever'
- Israeli education system described as facing 'complex emotional reality,' draining of 'security anxiety, collective trauma, tension' in classrooms

**Negative behavioral signals:**
- Family organized bat mitzvah celebration for Hodia after house destroyed by missile; community members volunteered DJ, machines, grandmother's wig; event was 'very moving'
- Survey of 18,000 Israelis returning from abroad: 60% feel safer in Israel than destination they were visiting
- Over 50 new immigrants from France and Britain landed at Ben Gurion during missile barrages; ~1,000 Jews worldwide opened aliyah files recently

**Behavioral signs with no evidence today:**
- A narrative of successful coping is visible and circulating (residents/officials describe coping as effective)
- The coping story reflects the entire population, not just a subset
- Residents express that the authority's narrative is credible and reflects their lived reality

### 📡 Information, Communication, and Sharing
*מידע, תקשורת ושיתוף*

**Score:** 7/10 — 🟢 Good &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Confidence:** medium &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Signals:** 6

Authorities are actively issuing guidance: the Home Front Command spokesman publicly addressed the 'fatal mistake' of not pre-planning shelter entry before sleep, and the Ramat Gan mayor sent direct messaging to residents with mobility difficulties. The IDF's Arabic-language spokesperson issued detailed evacuation warnings with mapped clearance zones, indicating some adaptation to different population groups. However, residents report confusion on the ground — one described a shorter-than-usual alert by saying 'we thought everything was destroyed... we didn't think it was above us' — suggesting that while guidance is being issued, it is not consistently penetrating or being understood by all residents. No direct evidence was captured of residents affirming that they feel informed or that official communication fully addresses their functional needs.

**Positive behavioral signals:**

**Negative behavioral signals:**

### 🛡️ Effective Life-Saving Behavior
*התנהגות אפקטיבית להצלת חיים*

**Score:** 1/10 — 🔴 Critical &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Confidence:** high &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Signals:** 26

The evidence paints a picture of life-saving behavior breaking down under the weight of sustained, repeated alerts. In a single 24-hour period, 790 people were injured while attempting to reach shelters, 30 were treated for anxiety casualties, and 3 were killed and 19 injured in road accidents triggered by alert-panic — demonstrating that the protective behavior itself is generating casualties. Two residents were killed in their living room because they 'apparently did not have time to reach' their protected room, and a 71-year-old lost consciousness and required CPR while descending to a shelter. At least 63 children have been injured in unintentional accidents since the campaign began, four fatally, indicating that the broader population — including caregivers — is struggling to execute protective behaviors safely. While some residents report sleeping in the protected room and guidance instructs compliance, the aggregate injury and casualty data reflects a population whose physical and psychological capacity to follow guidelines is being eroded by repetition and panic.

**Positive behavioral signals:**

### ⚙️ Functional Continuity
*רציפות תפקודית*

**Score:** 1/10 — 🔴 Critical &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Confidence:** high &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Signals:** 18

Functional continuity is severely degraded across multiple domains. Three million children have been confined to homes for over two and a half weeks, with the education minister absent from parliamentary oversight meetings and Zoom instruction failing for those without devices. The Savidor train station sustained a missile hit and saw daily passenger volume fall from 50,000 to approximately 20,000, with platform closures and days of electrical repair ahead. Essential services are strained at the margins: Eilat's emergency department faces staffing shortages, water boiling orders have been issued in five communities, and social workers report a dramatic increase in violence and economic distress with state attention focused elsewhere. Isolated signs of continuity exist — rail service resumed by 6 AM after overnight repairs, and a jazz venue held a performance for 50 attendees — but these are exceptions against a backdrop of widespread disruption rather than evidence of systemic recovery.

### 🤝 Community Capital and Resources
*הון ומשאבי קהילה*

**Score:** 7/10 — 🟢 Good &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Confidence:** high &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Signals:** 12

Community capital is mobilizing at multiple levels: the JNF activated 6 million shekels to move border families into hotels for Passover, the Toronto Jewish Federation and UJA committed nearly $2 million in emergency coordination funding, and municipal governments in Ashdod and Kiryat Gat are proactively issuing full refunds for suspended educational services. Technological resources are also being adapted, with Waze deploying a dedicated 'Crisis' button to locate shelters. However, serious gaps persist where community capital is not reaching the most vulnerable: tens of thousands of people with disabilities remain without shelter solutions, crane operators work fully exposed at height without any institutional response, and Holocaust survivors are reportedly giving up on accessing healthcare. The volunteering and resource mobilization that is occurring is real and significant, but it is not yet scaled or targeted to meet the needs of the most at-risk populations.
