## Executive Summary

On March 19, the dominant behavioral reality remains one of acute, sustained distress now entering its fourth week of Operation Lions Roar. Residents are demanding sleeping pills in sharply rising numbers, northern families who renovated their homes and returned are fleeing again, children are developing 'shelter flu' from prolonged stays in crowded unventilated bunkers, and 3,924 people have been evacuated to hospitals since the operation began. Yet beneath this strain, a striking behavioral split runs through every component: community capital and leadership are performing at their highest observed levels — pharmacists proactively scanning WhatsApp groups to deliver medications to the elderly, air defense recruitment nearly doubling, and the Home Front Command chief physically attending missile strike sites to demonstrate the value of shelter compliance — while narrative coherence, functional continuity, and wellbeing support for at-risk populations remain at or near their lowest observed scores. The population is not passive, but the gap between what civil society is generating from below and what state systems are delivering from above has widened.
