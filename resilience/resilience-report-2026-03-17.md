## Executive Summary

The population is living through sustained psychological strain — three weeks of nightly sirens, sleep deprivation, and acute fear responses are the dominant behavioral reality. Residents are rushing to shelters and sustaining injuries in the process (754 injured en route to shelters, 3 killed in traffic incidents during alerts); some, like one elderly woman blocked from a shelter, have stopped seeking protective cover altogether. Daily life structures have largely collapsed: all private daycares and kindergartens are closed by order, a quarter of businesses are shut or partially operating, and vulnerable populations — including teenagers in eating disorder treatment who were abruptly discharged — are deteriorating without their support frameworks. The narrative circulating is not one of effective coping but of fear, exhaustion, and institutional abandonment, punctuated by isolated voices of defiance such as the northern farmer who declares 'we will not leave even at the price of death.'

## Detailed Analysis

### 📖 Narrative
*נרטיב*

**Score:** 2/10 — 🔴 Critical &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Confidence:** high &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Signals:** 11

The dominant narrative circulating among residents is one of fear, exhaustion, and deterioration rather than effective coping — a mother describes her daughter being abruptly discharged from eating disorder day care at the war's start and rapidly sinking into depression, while population-level data shows a third of drivers experiencing excessive drowsiness and a quarter showing PTSD signs after three weeks of nightly sirens. A regional council head explicitly contradicts official reassurances, stating 'they did not tell us the full truth' about security conditions, signaling that authority narratives are not credible to those living on the front lines. Isolated counter-narratives of resilience do circulate — a farmer vowing never to leave, volunteers describing Israeli resilience as 'simply inspiring' — but these are voiced by a distinct subset rather than reflecting a broadly shared coping story. No evidence was found that officials are actively promoting or that residents widely accept a narrative of successful collective coping.

**Positive behavioral signals:**
- Mother of 15-year-old with anorexia: 'When the war started she was discharged from day care treatment. In the first days deterioration was rapid. She sank into depression, stopped getting out of bed and sometimes refused to take pills.'
- Regional council head: 'They told residents Hezbollah is deterred and we are returning to safe area - they did not tell us the full truth.'
- 38% of drivers report excessive drowsiness during war, about a quarter show signs of PTSD, 17% suffer from anxiety.
- Third week that Israel is not sleeping at night to sound of red alert warnings, sirens and psychological stress.

**Negative behavioral signals:**
- Farmer despite risk: 'We will not leave even at the price of death'
- Young volunteers: 'We see the resilience that Israeli people have, it is simply inspiring.'
- North American teenager: 'I understood we have how to defend ourselves - and I calmed down.'

**Behavioral signs with no evidence today:**
- A narrative of successful coping is visible and circulating (residents/officials describe coping as effective)
- Residents express that the authority's narrative is credible and reflects their lived reality

### 📡 Information, Communication, and Sharing
*מידע, תקשורת ושיתוף*

**Score:** 6/10 — 🟡 Moderate &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Confidence:** high &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Signals:** 10

Authorities are issuing directives and adapting communication to specific groups — the Ministry of Education has released targeted guidance protecting students from grade penalties, and the Health Ministry is directing hospital managers to review staff leave — but these messages are not resolving functional uncertainty among the public. Surveys of over 17,000 parents show 60% remain uncertain about school reopening, and the National Parents' Leadership chair is publicly demanding that 'the state must create a clear and stable emergency framework,' indicating that official communications are not meeting parents' needs. The gap between directives issued and decisions made by families is wide: 67% of parents say they prefer children not return to school even in the days before Passover vacation, and nearly half say they would avoid sending children even if permitted. The evidence shows information is being produced by authorities but is not translating into clarity or confidence for the population making daily decisions.

**Positive behavioral signals:**
- 60% of parents uncertain about school reopening across two large surveys of over 17,000 parents.
- National Parents' Leadership chair: 'Israeli parents cannot remain alone between work, children and security situation - the state must create a clear and stable emergency framework.'
- Ministry of Education directive issued that attendance will not count toward grades during the emergency period.
- Following intensive dialogue with Student and Youth Council: 'Students can breathe easy. A student who experienced a night of sirens or emotional difficulty should not be harmed in grades.'

**Negative behavioral signals:**
- 67% of parents prefer children not return to classes in days remaining before Passover, with nearly half saying they would avoid sending children even if permitted — suggesting official guidance is not resolving their uncertainty.
- Health Ministry official notes public has 'learned to get help remotely,' with over 21,000 calls to support hotlines, indicating some information channels are being used.

**Behavioral signs with no evidence today:**
- Residents state they receive the information they need to function during the emergency
- Residents express trust in information received from the authority
- Population perceives national media information as relevant and addressing their needs

### 🛡️ Effective Life-Saving Behavior
*התנהגות אפקטיבית להצלת חיים*

**Score:** 1/10 — 🔴 Critical &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Confidence:** high &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Signals:** 11

The behavioral data on life-saving compliance is alarming: 754 people have been injured en route to shelters and 3 killed in traffic incidents during alerts, pointing to chaotic and unsafe movement during warnings rather than orderly protective behavior. Non-compliance is explicitly documented — residents are lighting cigarettes inside sealed shelters despite this endangering children, and at least one elderly woman has stopped seeking shelter entirely after a traumatic experience of being turned away at a shelter door. The shelter exclusion incident itself — where residents describe 'a very shocking moment' of being blocked from entering a shelter during an active alert — represents a system failure that is actively deterring the behavior it is designed to encourage. While some shelter use is evidenced (visiting teenagers descending into shelters), no evidence was found of community-level preparedness promotion or widespread demonstrated knowledge of Home Front Command guidelines.

**Positive behavioral signals:**

- People lighting cigarettes inside shelters during alerts — violating safety rules and endangering children and adults.
- Regional council head: 'They told residents Hezbollah is deterred and we are returning to safe area - they did not tell us the full truth.'

**Negative behavioral signals:**

- Director of National Road Safety Authority calls to drivers: 'Don't rush to the shoulder, don't endanger others' — indicating official guidance exists, even if compliance is poor.

**Behavioral signs with no evidence today:**
- Residents are observed or reported to actually follow Home Front Command (HFC) protective guidelines (sheltering, evacuating, etc.)
- Residents demonstrate knowledge and understanding of HFC guidelines

### ⚙️ Functional Continuity
*רציפות תפקודית*

**Score:** 2/10 — 🔴 Critical &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Confidence:** high &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Signals:** 16

Functional continuity has broken down across multiple sectors simultaneously: all private daycares and kindergartens are closed by order, a quarter of businesses are shut or operating at reduced capacity, and a train station was temporarily closed after shrapnel damage — with no sign that these disruptions are resolving. Parents face a compounding economic trap, paying thousands of shekels monthly for closed childcare facilities while simultaneously losing income and scrambling for alternative care, a dynamic described as producing 'new poor people, war poor.' Healthcare is the clearest domain of maintained function — the health system transitioned to full emergency mode within hours of the first alerts and continues operating, including maintaining dialysis continuity and delivering babies during active alerts — but it is doing so under extreme load (3,530 hospital admissions) with staff leaves under review. Partial reopenings of educational frameworks in specific councils (Gilboa, Jerusalem special education) represent fragmented and inconsistent resumption rather than systemic continuity.

**Positive behavioral signals:**
- All private day care centers and kindergartens closed by Home Front Command order since start of war with Iran.
- About a quarter of Israeli businesses closed or operating partially; restaurants and small businesses hit hardest.
- Parents continue paying thousands of shekels monthly for childcare centers that are closed — children remain at home while parents cannot work.
- Shrapnel hit Holon Junction train station during Iranian fire; station closed temporarily.
- Health system operating in protected underground format as 'mega-casualty event' contingency; 3,530 wounded admitted to hospitals since operation start.
- Yossi Ambulance team operates around the clock to ensure dialysis patients reach life-saving treatment at Rambam Hospital.

**Negative behavioral signals:**
- Gilboa Regional Council announced educational frameworks will reopen in limited format from tomorrow.
- Special education institutions in Jerusalem set to return after 2.5 weeks of closure.
- Essential workers continue operating ports, roads, factories and infrastructure: 'Without them the state simply does not function.'

**Behavioral signs with no evidence today:**
- Residents succeed in managing daily life (reported continuation of work, commerce, or social roles)
- Residents report that sufficient resources are available to maintain routine functioning

### 🤝 Community Capital and Resources
*הון ומשאבי קהילה*

**Score:** 9/10 — 🟢 Strong &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Confidence:** high &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Signals:** 11

Community capital is robustly activated across multiple sectors: hundreds of diaspora volunteers are physically present in Israel packing food and cleaning shelters, Latet's network of 210 partner organizations is sustaining national food distribution, and El Al is running a large-scale repatriation operation at reduced cost. Municipalities are innovating at the local level — Ramat Gan deployed a Technion mobile science unit near protected spaces to serve children locked out of school — while national bodies are investing in long-term resilience infrastructure, including a new 5-million-shekel research lab in Sderot. The main stress fracture in community capital is resource depletion: aid organizations are absorbing new populations (reservists, Nova survivors) while donations are declining, and the coordination failure around foreign construction workers — where every relevant authority deflects responsibility — reveals that capital mobilization is uneven and excludes the most vulnerable. Overall, the breadth of voluntary and cross-sector action is a genuine strength even as resource gaps widen.

**Positive behavioral signals:**
- Hundreds of young diaspora Jews volunteer packing food bundles for the needy, cleaning and arranging shelters for residents while dealing with missile threat they never knew before.
- Aid organization Latet operates food bank system through network of 210 partner organizations in national coverage.
- El Al leads evacuation operation for tens of thousands of Israelis stranded abroad, staging tens of thousands at no cost and setting reduced return flight prices.
- About 4,000 citizens evacuated from damaged homes to hotels; National Emergency Authority reports operating a faster evacuation mechanism.
- Ramat Gan municipality brings Technion innovation mobile unit offering science activities near protected spaces for grades 3-6.
- Innovation Authority establishing national research lab for human resilience in Sderot with 5 million shekel investment.

**Negative behavioral signals:**
- Aid organizations report increase in assistance requests alongside reduction in resources due to decline in donations.
- Knesset Foreign Workers Committee reveals coordination failure: 'Complete abandonment' of foreign workers on construction sites, with no body taking responsibility.
