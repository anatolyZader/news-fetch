- **URL:** (audio recording)
- **Published:** 2026-04-13
- **Source:** kan-reka — מיקרופון פתוח כאן רקע 11:05-12:00

[Scene type: expert_interview | Speakers: expert B, caller (unidentified male)]

The expert explains the physiological mechanism underlying ankle swelling in older adults: veins that return blood from the lower body to the heart function less efficiently with age, making it harder for blood to move upward against gravity. This causes fluid accumulation (edema) when legs remain in a dependent position for extended periods. She notes that the caller's behavior of elevating his legs at home is an appropriate compensatory strategy to reduce swelling by facilitating venous return.

Key quotes:
- "These veins that conduct blood upward work worse with age, and blood finds it difficult to move upward against gravity, and therefore swelling can occur when a person stands or keeps legs down for a long time"
- "What you do—lying down and raising your legs a little—can help"
